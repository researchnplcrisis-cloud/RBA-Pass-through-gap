I downloaded RBA Statistical Tables F1, F1.1, F4, F5 and F6 and used monthly observations from Jan 2020 to Sep 2026. F1.1 supplies the monthly-average cash-rate target (F1 daily dates each decision); F5 the banks' advertised standard variable owner-occupier housing rate; F6 the average rates actually charged on existing and newly funded owner-occupier variable-rate loans across all institutions (latest Aug 2026); F4 online savings and banks' $10,000 one-year term deposit rates. Pass-through is the change in each retail rate divided by the change in the cash-rate target, from the month before the first hike to the episode end, or each series' latest month, with one- and three-month lags checked per hike. Monthly averages split mid-month decisions across two months, so single-hike figures are noisy. Gaps are left as gaps. Caveat: F4 and F5 are advertised rates; F6 averages hide wide dispersion across borrowers and lenders.
